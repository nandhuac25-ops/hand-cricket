## Selected Direction — Floodlight Folklore

### Design Movement
**Contemporary sports broadcast design informed by Indian street-cricket nostalgia.** The experience should feel like a treasured hand game has been promoted into a high-stakes night match, not transformed into a generic technology dashboard.

### Core Principles
1. **The match is the stage.** Score, hands, and the next move retain visual priority over product navigation.
2. **Tactility over gloss.** Grain, paper-like texture, pitch lines, and hand-drawn gesture contours keep the game warm and physical.
3. **Floodlight contrast.** Large pools of warm light emerge from a deep charcoal arena, giving every choice a dramatic focus.
4. **Readable at speed.** Every match state has one decisive focal message, supported by quiet metadata and unmistakable action buttons.

### Color Philosophy
The palette begins with **Night Pitch**, a dark blue-black that makes the screen feel like an evening ground rather than an empty black interface. **Signal Lime** is the ownable action color: energetic, sporty, and more distinctive than predictable electric blue. A muted **tangerine umpire light** marks baseball and decisive outcomes; warm ivory provides a familiar paper-and-jersey counterpoint. Light is used as a functional cue—brightest on the active play and the number to choose.

### Layout Paradigm
The interface is arranged as a **vertical match poster**: a slim broadcast mast at the top, a tall active-arena middle, and a grounded number tray at the bottom. Desktop uses slanted score slates that lean into the central arena, while mobile collapses the same scene into stacked broadcast bands. Setup screens retain the arena backdrop and place choices like match tickets on an angled rail rather than in a centred dashboard grid.

### Signature Elements
1. **Floodlight bloom:** restrained radial light pools and dust motes behind active match elements.
2. **Score slates:** skewed, borderless panels with thin lime rules, as if clipped from a live broadcast.
3. **Gesture cards:** illustrated hand silhouettes with a concentric number badge, animated as they enter and retreat from the arena.

### Interaction Philosophy
Each action should communicate the logic of a real hand match: selecting a number feels like committing a gesture, and a revealed opponent value lands as a moment of suspense. Controls respond with a compact press, glow, and directional movement. Navigation is always reversible with a clearly labeled Back control; no feature should strand the player.

### Animation
Page transitions use 220–360ms directional wipes, like a broadcast camera cutting to a new moment. During a turn, the two hands translate toward the centre, the number badges resolve, then both hands ease back after the result. Score updates rise and settle with a brief lime flash; OUT uses a short high-contrast umpire-light pulse rather than continuous shaking. Ambient floodlight particles drift only when reduced motion is not requested. All number buttons use transform/opacity transitions with a 120–160ms active response.

### Typography System
**Barlow Condensed** is the all-caps match voice: headlines, scores, innings labels, and kinetic result states use its wide, athletic forms at weight 600–800. **DM Sans** handles game rules, body copy, player labels, and controls at 400–700. A score is always significantly larger than nearby labels, while instructions stay sentence-case and calm.

### Brand Essence
**HAND PLAY turns the hand games people grew up playing into a tense, tactile night-match experience for friends, families, and quick competitors.**  
**Personality:** spirited, tactile, competitive.

### Brand Voice
Headlines are direct, slightly dramatic, and grounded in match language. CTAs are commands that move the player into play, while microcopy explains the state without technical jargon.  
Example headline: **“MAKE YOUR MOVE. OWN THE MATCH.”**  
Example microcopy: **“Same number? The batter walks.”**

### Wordmark & Logo
The mark is a bold, circular **hand-and-ball crest**: three extended finger strokes curve around a small ball, leaving a single energetic gap. The accompanying wordmark is a custom-feeling condensed lockup with a clipped diagonal in the A, echoing a scoreboard tally.

### Signature Brand Color
**Signal Lime — #C8F447.**

## Style Decisions

- Every match state must carry at least two street-cricket cues: a chalk crease, paper-scorecard texture, taped-ball mark, dust motes, or sun-faded print detail.
- The mast, score slates, and utility controls use the same clipped broadcast-hardware language; circular generic app controls are avoided.
- The number tray is a signature element: each number is an angled match ticket with bold Barlow Condensed numerals. Signal Lime appears only on the active action, primary command, or decisive scoring message.
